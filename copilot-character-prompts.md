# Copilot Character Prompts – German Night Thriller Cast

Paste the **STYLE LOCK** at the end of every prompt, word for word. Use the same character block word for word every time a character comes back (that stops the faces drifting between episodes). Every character is an adult, 25 or older.

---

## STYLE LOCK
```
Graphic novel illustration, clean confident ink linework with subtle crosshatching, muted earthy palette, warm amber lamplight contrasted with cool blue-grey shadows, textured aged-parchment beige background, cinematic moody lighting, realistic adult proportions and anatomy, mature European faces with natural wrinkles and skin texture, consistent character design across all panels, no text, no letters, no watermark, no logo
```

## SHEET TEMPLATES
- **Full sheet (main cast):** `Character reference sheet, 6 panels in a 3x2 grid separated by thin cream borders, the exact same person in every panel, shoulders-up portraits. Panels: front neutral, three-quarter left suspicious, three-quarter right thoughtful, left profile, looking up worried, looking down in grief. [CHARACTER BLOCK] [STYLE LOCK]`
- **Mini sheet (supporting cast):** `Character reference sheet, 2 panels side by side, the exact same person in both, shoulders-up. Panel 1 front neutral, panel 2 three-quarter view with [EMOTION]. [CHARACTER BLOCK] [STYLE LOCK]`
- **Scene shot:** `Wide cinematic 16:9 scene. [CHARACTER BLOCK] [ACTION], in [LOCATION], at night, [WEATHER]. Character placed in the right third of the frame, calmer darker space on the left for subtitles. [STYLE LOCK]`

## Copilot tips
- **Faces drifting?** Generate the 6-panel sheet once, then upload it and add "use the uploaded reference sheet for the face and clothing exactly."
- **Prompt blocked?** Swap words like "blood", "body", "kill" and "gun" for "dark stain", "figure lying still", "danger" and "something in his hand". Stick to implied threat: shadows, a door left ajar, a letter.
- **Too many people in a shot?** Copilot mixes up faces past two characters. Keep it to two at most and do crowds as silhouettes.
- **Thirty-minute episode:** plan for about 120–150 images. Generate 5–6 base scenes per location and change the camera angle ("close-up of hands", "over-the-shoulder", "from the doorway").
