## Pinned comment

> Everest's summit rock is Ordovician limestone (~450–470 million years old), with fragments of trilobites, sea lilies and shells. If you could ask Mallory and Irvine one question, what would it be?

Ready answers:
- *"Real seashells?"* — Fossil fragments of shelled sea animals (brachiopods), plus sea lilies and trilobites, in the limestone of the top ~125 m.
- *"How did they get up there?"* — India collided with Asia and folded the old seafloor upward into the Himalaya.
- *"Is it really still growing?"* — GPS shows about 2 mm a year; part of that comes from a nearby river eroding a gorge so the crust rebounds (Nature Geoscience, 2024).
- *"Was it really Irvine's foot?"* — National Geographic's team found a boot and a sock labelled "A.C. IRVINE" in 2024; DNA testing was announced to confirm it.
- *"Did they reach the top?"* — Nobody knows. Their Kodak camera, which might hold the answer, has never been found.
- *"Are these real photos?"* — No, they're AI illustrations checked for accuracy; the facts are sourced above.
