## Pinned comment

> The tree was later nicknamed "Prometheus". Its loss helped push for protecting bristlecones (Great Basin National Park was created in 1986). Methuselah's exact spot still isn't public. Would you go looking for it?

Ready answers:
- *"Why did they cut it down?"* — Accounts differ (a stuck or broken coring tool is the most-told version); the Forest Service approved it so he could get a full cross-section.
- *"Is Methuselah still the oldest?"* — It's the oldest known tree with a widely accepted age (~4,850 years). In 2012 an even older bristlecone (~5,060 years) was reported, but it hasn't been independently verified.
- *"How can 4,844 rings mean ~4,900 years?"* — In very harsh years a bristlecone can skip growing a ring, so the true age is a bit higher than the count.
- *"Is the location secret because of Prometheus?"* — The official reason is protection from vandalism; "can you blame them?" is the video's wink, not the Forest Service's words.
