## Pinned comment

> The best part: scientists measure this with DNA from millions of cells of one tissue type at a time (one cell doesn't hold enough carbon-14), using an accelerator mass spectrometer. Which result surprised you most: fat, heart, brain, or eye?

Ready answers:
- *"So I'm radioactive?"* — You always were, a little: natural carbon-14 and potassium-40 are in everyone. The tests added a small extra amount. Today's dose from all test fallout is about 0.005 mSv a year, roughly 1/500 of the natural radiation you get anyway (UNSCEAR).
- *"Isn't the whole body replaced every 7 years?"* — That's a myth. Some tissues renew fast (gut lining in days, red blood cells in about 4 months); others barely or never (cortex neurons, the lens core, tooth enamel).
- *"Does this still work for young people?"* — Less and less. By 2019 the bomb signal in the air had faded to about 1 % above normal, so cells made today are much harder to date this way. Science got a once-only clock, and it's running out.
- *"Why does the lens never renew?"* — Lens fibre cells give up their nuclei to stay transparent. The lens grows by adding new layers on top, like tree rings, so the first ones stay in the centre for life.
- *"How exact is it?"* — It dates the average birth year of a tissue's cells, typically to within a year or two; it can't date one single cell.
