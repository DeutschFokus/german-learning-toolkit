## Pinned comment

> Voyager 1 still listens: commands from Earth now take about a day to arrive, and the delay grows by roughly half an hour every year as it flies away at about 17 km/s. If you could send it one sentence, what would you say?

Ready answers:
- *"Is it exactly 24 hours?"* — From November 18, 2026, yes: light (and radio) needs one day to cover the distance. It keeps growing by about 30 minutes a year.
- *"How can 22 watts reach us?"* — Huge 70 m dishes and extremely sensitive receivers in NASA's Deep Space Network. The signal is unimaginably faint and the data rate is tiny, but it still gets through.
- *"When will it go silent?"* — Its nuclear power source loses about 4 watts a year. In 2026 only two science instruments are still on, and NASA hopes to keep it going into the early 2030s.
- *"Is the love story real?"* — Yes. Ann Druyan described recording an hour of her brainwaves on June 3, 1977, two days after she and Carl Sagan decided to marry; it was compressed into about a minute on the record. They married and stayed together until his death in 1996.
- *"What's actually on the record?"* — 115 images, greetings in 55 languages, a montage of Earth's sounds (including a heartbeat) and 90 minutes of music. NASA has shared the contents online.
- *"Will it hit the star?"* — No. "Drift past" means passing within about 1.6 light-years of Gliese 445, a red dwarf, in about 40,000 years.
