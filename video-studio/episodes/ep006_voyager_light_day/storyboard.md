## 1. Topic selection

EP005 showed what lifts the scores: a personal hook, a human story, a revelation the viewer feels. EP006 adds a fourth lever that no evergreen topic has: **timeliness**. On **November 18, 2026** (12 days after this episode was made) Voyager 1 becomes the first human-made object one light-day from Earth. **Winner: #1, by the widest margin yet.** The hook is personal ("say hello now, it hears you tomorrow"); the news peg is real and imminent; and inside the facts there is an untold love story (Ann Druyan's brainwaves, recorded two days after she and Carl Sagan decided to marry) that turns a space fact into something people feel and forward.

## 2. Story architecture

| Window | Job | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 s | Hook | Frame 0: "SAY HELLO NOW, / IT HEARS YOU / TOMORROW" over Voyager 1 against the Milky Way; radio waves carrying a "hello" roll up from the Sun's direction, and a countdown shows when it would arrive. |
| 6–17 s | Mystery → the milestone | The 1977 launch. Then the whole solar system: a pulse of light leaves home and needs a full 24 hours to reach Voyager — November 18, 2026, one light-day, 25.9 billion km. |
| 17–28 s | Discoveries | It still calls home on about 22 W (a fridge bulb), heard by 70 m dishes. In 1990 it turned around for the pale blue dot: Earth, 0.12 pixel. |
| 28–49 s | Revelation | The golden record: 90 minutes of music, greetings in 55 languages, a heartbeat… and the brainwaves of a woman who had just fallen in love: Ann Druyan; the man she loved, Carl Sagan, led the project. |
| 49–60 s | Payoff + loop | In 40,000 years it drifts past another star (Gliese 445), still carrying their love story. "And for now… it's still listening." → loops into "If you said hello to this spacecraft right now…" |
