EP002 — THERE'S A HOLE IN YOUR VISION RIGHT NOW
Recording-ready voiceover script (deep, calm male narrator)
========================================================================
Video length: 60 s   |   Spoken words: 144   |   Narration window: 00:00.08 – 00:59.40

THE VOICE
  Intelligent, calm, quietly fascinated. Authority without lecturing; a close-mic,
  one-to-one conversation, not a trailer. Deep does not mean slow: keep momentum
  (about 165–175 words per minute inside sentences) and let the PAUSES do the drama.
  Let a slight smile in on the lighter lines. Never push or shout.

MARKS
  CAPS      = lean on this word (pitch or weight, not volume)
  /         = short breath, about 0.2–0.4 s
  //        = beat, about 0.4–0.8 s
  ///       = let it land, 0.8 s or more
  ...       = suspend the pitch: the thought continues

HOW TO RECORD
  1. Quiet, soft room (closet or duvet fort beats a bare room). Mic 15–20 cm away,
     slightly off-axis to avoid plosives. Peaks around -10 dBFS, never above -3.
  2. Record the whole script in one take as WAV (48 kHz, 24-bit, mono).
     Leave 2 s of silence at the start. Do a second full take if a line slips.
  3. The timings below are a GUIDE from the AI reference read, not a click track.
     Read naturally. The pipeline aligns your take word by word, keeps your voice
     at its natural speed, and re-times the scenes, captions and sound design to it.
  4. If your read runs long, the pipeline tightens the pauses first. If it still
     overruns 60 s it tells you which lines to trim; it never speeds your voice up.
  5. Then run:  python3 production/build.py human path/to/your_take.wav

CLEAN READ (no marks) — for a first run-through
========================================================================
  There's a hole in your vision. Right now. Want to find it? Close your
  left eye. Stare at the cross. Now slowly bring your phone closer...
  until the dot disappears. That's your blind spot: where your optic
  nerve leaves the eye, nothing detects light. You never notice, because
  your brain paints over it. And that's not the only edit. You blink
  about fifteen times a minute, and never see the darkness. Your eyes
  jump about three times a second. Even now, reading this. Each jump
  smears the image, so your brain cuts the blur... then covers the gap
  by stretching the next image back in time. That's why, when you glance
  at a clock, the second hand seems to freeze. It didn't. Your brain
  stretched it. You're not seeing the world live. You're seeing an edit.
  One so good, you never noticed the hole...
