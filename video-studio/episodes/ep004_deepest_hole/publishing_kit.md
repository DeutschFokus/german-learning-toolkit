## Pinned comment

> Drilling started on 24 May 1970 and the 15 km target was never reached: at 12,262 m the rock was about 180 °C and so pliable the hole kept closing. Nothing has been drilled deeper since. What would YOU have wanted them to find down there?

Ready answers:
- *"Isn't there a deeper hole now?"* — Some oil wells are longer (more than 12 km), but they run mostly sideways. Kola is still the deepest in straight-down (true vertical) depth. China's Shendi Take-1 reached 10,910 m vertically in February 2025: the second deepest, still shallower than Kola.
- *"Did they hear screams from hell?"* — No. The "Well to Hell" story is a well-documented hoax that spread through Christian broadcasters in the late 1980s and 1990s; the "screams" recording was assembled from sound effects. Wrong place, too: it was usually set in Siberia.
- *"Why couldn't they just keep going?"* — Heat. At around 180 °C the rock behaved plastically and the hole deformed whenever the bit came out; equipment and money ran out too.
- *"Is it really narrower than a plate?"* — The borehole is about 23 cm (9 in) across; a dinner plate is about 25–28 cm.
- *"Is 0.2 % right?"* — 12.262 km ÷ 6,371 km (Earth's mean radius) = 0.19 %, rounded to 0.2 %.
- *"Is the water claim real?"* — Yes: at several kilometres down the granite was heavily fractured and saturated with water, which surprised the team.
